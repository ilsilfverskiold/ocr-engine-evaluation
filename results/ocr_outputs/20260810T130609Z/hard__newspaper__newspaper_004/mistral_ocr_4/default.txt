# Group hopes to snuff out underage tobacco purchases

By Jessica Lowell
Young Tobacco

CHEYENNE - The lure of the forbidden is a powerful bait.

And all the laws in the world can't prevent a kid bent or buying cigarettes from trying.

Sherri Parker, 16, of Cheyenne, knows a few ways around the system; she's been smoking for half her life, but there and when her family lived in Japan.

She'll borrow a sister's identification, or if she finds a store that doesn't card her she'll go back again and again.

Parker, and other youths like her, are the target of anti-smoking campaigns, as well as federal and state laws aimed at severing kids from nicotine.

They're also the target here of the Wyoming Coalition for Responsible Tobacco Retailing.

That group, made up of retail and wholesale industry groups, is planning a series of workshops in the state in the next two weeks to help merchants train employees to comply with the law that forbids anyone under 18 from buying tobacco.

Justin Jones, a member of the Wyoming Retail Merchants Association, said the seminars were first held two years ago.

In the wake of the classes, the number of tobacco sales to minors dropped. Last year, when no seminars were held, that number spiked, Jones said.

The groups hope by reviving the sessions the number of sales will drop once again. The Cheyenne session will be held from 10 a.m. to noon today at Little America. Walk-ins are welcome, Jones said.
